@@text
**CompTIA A+** is the most common first certification for IT support jobs. It's vendor-neutral, recognized worldwide (including by foreign-friendly employers in Japan), and it proves you know the fundamentals across hardware, operating systems, networking, security and troubleshooting.

## The two exams

You need to pass **both**. The current series is **220-1201 (Core 1)** and **220-1202 (Core 2)**, launched in 2025. Always check the official objectives on comptia.org before booking: they list every topic that can appear.

| Core 1 | Core 2 |
|---|---|
| Mobile devices | Operating systems (Windows, macOS, Linux) |
| Networking | Security |
| Hardware | Software troubleshooting |
| Virtualization and cloud | Operational procedures |
| Hardware and network troubleshooting | |

Each exam: up to **90 questions in 90 minutes**, multiple choice plus **performance-based questions** (PBQs: drag-and-drop, configure a router screen, put steps in order). The pass mark is **675** (Core 1) and **700** (Core 2) on a scale of 100–900. You can take it at a test centre (Pearson VUE, including in Tokyo and Osaka) or online.

## How to study with this app

1. Do the lessons in order. Every quiz question goes into the **Review → Practice exams** bank for this track.
2. Do flashcards **every day** in Review. Spaced repetition beats cramming.
3. Build the **home lab** (Foundations track) and do the Help Desk Triage build here. PBQs reward people who've actually clicked through Windows tools.
4. When you score **85%+** on practice exams several times in a row, book the real one.
5. A good plan: Core 1 in 4–6 weeks, Core 2 in 4–6 weeks, around an hour a day.

> Exam tip: CompTIA questions often ask for the **BEST** or **FIRST** step. Several answers may work; pick the one that follows the troubleshooting methodology, is least disruptive, and is safest for data.
